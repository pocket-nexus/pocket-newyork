# Repository instructions

- Pocket New York owns the export pages (`web/pocket/`), the scene compiler and the pack (`crates/ny-cook`, `crates/ny-pack`), what moves (`crates/ny-sim`), the interface (`ui/`, `crates/ny-interface`), the device profiles (`profiles/`) and the runtimes (`vita/`). The rest of `web/` is the model: Slop New York by mrdoob, under its own MIT notice (`web/LICENSE`); keep that notice and do not edit the model's files. PocketJS (pinned in `vendor/pocketjs`) owns the device toolchains, the Vita dev host and wired debug transport, VPK packaging, the GXM device kernel, the Pocket3D title card and icon.
- Do not edit the submodule to fix application behavior. Send reusable changes to PocketJS, then update the pinned revision here.
- three.js is pinned: `THREE_COMMIT` in `tools/web.ts` (the dev branch when the reference was last pushed). `bun tools/newyork.ts three` downloads it into the ignored `web/vendor/three`, and `serve` answers the reference's CDN imports from there. The reference's `index.html` stays as it is.
- The look of a surface is defined once, by the reference's builders and materials. The export page runs them and records what they produce: the scene graph (`web/pocket/scene.js`, with the instances its streaming systems make anywhere in the city), every vertex's colour as the material's own node graph computes it (`probe.js`), and pictures its materials draw (bakes). The compiler lowers that. Do not re-implement a material in the compiler or in a device shader: change how the export draws it, and export again. A material the compiler cannot lower is named in its receipt, not drawn in a colour of its own.
- A change to the export, the compiler or a profile goes through `bun tools/newyork.ts export` and `cook`; SceneIR, packs and receipts are outputs under `.pocket-build/`. A device difference is a profile value or a lowering in `crates/ny-cook`, not a branch in the export.
- The pack's tables and vertex layouts are `crates/ny-pack`; the compiler and every runtime read them from there. A layout change raises `VERSION`. Every vertex layout has a stride that is a multiple of 4 (`cargo test -p ny-pack`).
- `ny-sim` and `ny-pack` build without `std` (`default-features = false`): `core`, `alloc` and `libm` only. What moves (the player in its four modes, the cameras, the traffic, the hours) lives in `ny-sim` with a host test, and a device only draws it.
- `ny-sim`'s controllers, cameras, location label and signals are the reference's, checked against it: `web/pocket/sim.js` drives the reference's own objects with fixed scripts and records them (`.pocket-build/scene/ir/oracle/`), and `cargo test -p ny-sim` replays the scripts through the port over `.pocket-build/scene/sim.pack` (`bun tools/newyork.ts export --only sim`, then the compiler). A change there keeps those tests passing at their tolerances; a deliberate difference from the reference is written beside the code and in the README. The traffic is a reduction of the reference's (no lane changes, no pedestrians) and is checked by `tests/game.rs`.
- `bun tools/newyork.ts view` draws a pack in a browser tab (`web/pocket/view.html`) for looking at what the compiler made. It is not a device and does not measure one.
- Use Conventional Commits for commits and pull requests (`type(scope): summary`). Publish validated changes as a Draft PR.
- Keep captures, logs, SceneIR, packs, USB-share contents and build receipts in `.pocket-build/`. Put reproducible commands, results and limits in the PR description instead of committing them.
- Separate evidence kinds when reporting: host build and tests, compile receipt, reference capture, viewer capture, device frame timing (`bench`), device capture, and what a person saw on the console.
- The city data comes from NYC Open Data and OpenStreetMap (ODbL) through the reference: a compiled pack is a derived database of those sources and keeps their terms. Do not commit a build or a pack, and do not attach one to a GitHub release; device packages are published on Pocket Studio.
- The interface is one PocketJS app, `ui/`, drawn over the city. A device crate draws no text and no 2D. The procedure is `vendor/pocketjs/skills/pocket3d-interface/SKILL.md`. The Pocket3D title card plays first at every launch and the app icon is the Pocket3D icon from `vendor/pocketjs/engine/pocket3d/icon/`: this repository holds no icon file (`vendor/pocketjs/skills/pocket3d-brand/SKILL.md`).
- `ui/app/protocol.ts` and `crates/ny-interface` are the two sides of one protocol. Change both in one commit, with `cargo test -p ny-interface` and `bun tools/ui.ts test`. The flow (the title, play, the menu) is `ny_interface::Session` around `ny_sim::Game`; a device does not keep its own, and a device's pad reaches the game through `ny_interface::Pad`. Where the player is travels as names the device writes (`State::locate`); the interface does not compute a place.
- The names the interface shows of the reference's modes, hours, cameras and places, and its map of the island, are compiled by `tools/ui.ts` from the export (`world.json`, `interface.json`, `map.png`, written by `web/pocket/world.js` and `map.js` from the reference's own HUD and atlas): do not write one in `ui/`. A number that changes during play is a member of `t` written to its node through `@pocketjs/framework/hot`, never a signal; a timer goes through `createPulse` (the renderer counts its seconds), and a screen is wrapped in `Keep`. Look at every screen with `bun tools/ui.ts preview` before a device build.
